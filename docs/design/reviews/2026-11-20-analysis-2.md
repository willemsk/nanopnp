**The verdicts.** The author marked all 17 picks: 11 `keep`, 6 `change`, no `drop`. No keep carries a note. The

full outline. Three changes asked whether the 3D view was a mesh view. It is: the solved field on the solve's own
flat (r, z) mesh, drawn face-on by NGSolve's renderer in its rainbow colour map with the wireframe on. Asked what the
view should be, the author chose "Mesh now, 3D in tree" (WB-53). Round 1's five rows that this round does not show
stay in the store with `round: 1`, recorded by [round 1's collect](2026-11-20-analysis.md), and are not counted here.

**How it is built.** At the collect, after WP5.21's merge (WB-53):
- **The map drawn whole.** Beneath the pore's close-up, the map draws the whole domain on the close-up's scale
  (`map_drawing`'s `backdrop`), so the field fills the view past the pore. The view still opens on the pore, and the
  scale is still the 99th percentile of what the close-up draws (WB-49). A comparison's maps take the same backdrop
  over the fluids' overlap, on their close-up's two scales (`comparison_maps`' `scales`).
- **The outline.** The outline has a role of its own in the (r, z) view, 1 px wide with a 1 px halo where it was
  2 px with 3 px. It runs whole, to the domain's edge, over the field that now runs there too.
- **The charts above the numbers.** The explorer's tiles stand Field, Current against bias, Axial cut, then
  Quantities.
- **Mesh, not 3D.** The switch reads *Map · Mesh*, Comparison's view *Mesh*, and the caption says it is the field on
  the solve's mesh. A true 3D view, the (r, z) field turned about the axis and cut open, joins REV-268's design round
  as a plot type, with the library that would draw it.
